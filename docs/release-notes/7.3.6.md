# Nishizumi Paints 7.3.6 (experimental)

Release date: 2026-10-11

This is an experimental build. It makes `fetch.tradingpaints.gg` the first Trading Paints host the app asks, and it fixes a long list of bugs found by reviewing the whole program. Among them: files of your own that could be deleted or painted over, a background service that could crash in a loop on one locked file, random fallbacks that painted over real paints, online fallback settings that did nothing, a favorite random paint given to the whole grid, a mouse wheel that changed settings while scrolling, and an app restart that never came back. Please report anything odd.

## Trading Paints: the working host comes first

The app asks two Trading Paints hosts for each driver's paint list. `dl.tradingpaints.com` has been failing its secure connection since 7.3.4, so every start of the app first waited for that failure before switching to `fetch.tradingpaints.gg`. Now `fetch.tradingpaints.gg` is asked first and `dl.tradingpaints.com` is only the backup.

With the working host first, a team download from the Teams tab sent every request to the broken backup as well, even after the main host had answered all of them, which ended a successful download with failed connections and seconds of waiting. Only requests no host answered go to the backup now. **Download ALL team paints...** also asked the backup again for every batch that had no paints for your team.

During an outage the app also spends far fewer requests:

- A host that keeps answering with server errors or timeouts now moves to the back, as a host that refuses connections already did. Before, every driver of the pass paid all its retries on it.
- On a superspeedway track an outage cost 14 requests per driver. A failed `_ss` request now ends there, and the extra `fetch_user.php` lookup is skipped while its host is unreachable.

## The online fallback settings now work

In the **Downloads** settings, **Lane mode**, **Manual max lanes**, **Process cars, helmets, and suits together online**, and **Give timed-out online fallbacks one final online retry** were saved but never used: the online fallback handled one driver at a time. Now:

1. Each car model gets its own lane. **Safe** runs one lane at a time, **Manual** up to the number you set, and **Session Total** all of them at once. Drivers of the same car still go one after another inside their lane.
2. Helmets and suits run next to the car lanes with **Process together** on, and after them with it off.
3. With the final retry on, every paint whose download failed gets one more online pick. A car with nothing left to pick is not retried.
4. Whatever is still missing comes from the local RandomPool.

A driver now counts as done only when every paint they needed arrived, so a car that failed is no longer hidden by a helmet that worked.

## A favorite random paint is used once per session

A paint marked as favorite was given to every driver who needed a fallback on that car, so a whole grid could end up in the same paint. It is now used once per session: the first driver who needs a fallback on that car gets it, and the others get other paints. This applies to the local RandomPool, the online fallback and the Session tab's random buttons, and a favorite already used earlier in the same session counts as used.

## Your own paint files are left alone

- Closing the app after leaving a session deleted your own car, helmet, and suit even with **Keep my livery locally** on (the default).
- With **Sync my livery from server** off, the files of your own car and team are the ones you put there, but three things still replaced or deleted them:
  - a driver swap in your own team, or **Force refresh current car/team** on your own row, deleted them, and nothing was downloaded to replace them
  - the team-driver preload fallback copied a personal paint over your team livery
  - the iRacing UI previews put your Trading Paints `car_<id>.tga` back over your own file every 20 seconds

  All three now leave your files alone, and the previews show as paused until you turn syncing back on.
- With syncing on, a `car_<id>.tga` the app did not install is still replaced by the preview, as since 7.3.0, but it is now kept in paint history first, so **Restore previous** brings it back.
- AI roster cleanup deleted any folder in `Documents\iRacing\airosters` without a `roster.json`, including a backup folder of your own. It now removes only folders the app made, or empty ones.
- AI roster folder names come from Trading Paints, and a name with a backslash could write files outside `airosters`. Names are now always one folder inside it.
- A `settings.json` that cannot be read is copied aside as `settings.json.unreadable-<date>` before the app starts with default settings, instead of being overwritten by the next saved setting.

## The background service no longer crash-loops on one file

Several file steps of a session pass had no error handling. One locked or unwritable file, or a full disk, crashed the service loop: the paints saved in that pass were never cleaned up, and the restarted service downloaded the whole grid again and failed at the same place.

- A paint file that iRacing holds open is retried, as downloads already were, and a file that still fails is skipped instead of ending the pass.
- Copies into the RandomPool are written under a temporary name first. A copy cut short by a full disk or a shutdown used to keep the real name, and the pool then handed that broken `car.tga` to other drivers forever. A failed pool update no longer fails the pass.
- A hand-edited date such as `"n/a"` in the driver overrides or random preferences file made every pass fail. Bad values are now ignored.
- Replay packs: a locked file no longer crashes the restore, and a failed capture no longer deletes the live session's paints.
- Closing the app during a pass now cancels it, so the paints it already saved are cleaned up instead of staying in the paint folder.
- Closing the console window with its X, logging off or shutting Windows down now stops the console-only service properly, so the session's paints are cleaned up and the tray icon goes away. `Ctrl+Break` works like `Ctrl+C`.

## Only one copy of the app runs

A window attached to the console-only service started its own downloader when the console went away, so opening the app again could start a second full copy working on the same paint folder. The window now runs its own downloader only when no other copy is running.

## The mouse wheel scrolls the page, not the settings

On Windows the mouse wheel goes to the control under the pointer. Scrolling a settings page therefore changed every number box and drop-down the pointer passed over, and saved it. On the Random tab the **Cars (GB)** limit dropped by 0.5 GB per notch, and the next trim deleted paints from the pool. Number boxes, drop-downs and sliders now only react to the wheel after you click them; otherwise the wheel scrolls the page. Lists under the pointer also scrolled twice per notch and now scroll once.

## Fewer wrong random paints

- In online fallback mode, a driver whose own paint is a Custom Number (`car_num_<id>.tga`) got a random car, spec map, and decal on top of it.
- A driver who only needed a random helmet or suit used up a whole car set from the RandomPool, so a later driver who needed a car could get none.
- **Block** on a helmet or suit that came with a RandomPool set did nothing; the blocked helmet came back next session.
- A favorite online paint that could not be downloaded was tried again first for every later driver, each paying the full retries.
- **Rebuild from current files** put every car into the accessories bucket of the RandomPool, where no car is ever picked.
- Trading Paints lists some cars together under one showroom ID. All of them share one paint template except the **Super Formula SF23**, where the Honda and the Toyota use different ones, so a random SF23 paint could be made for the other car and not line up, and the RandomPool filed every SF23 paint under the Honda. Paints from that list are now only used when they are known to be for the car that needs one; otherwise the local pool is used.
- A car override (**Assign**) wrote the new car paint over a driver's Trading Paints paint but left that paint's spec map, decal and custom number, and on superspeedways the old `_ss` paint kept showing. The override now replaces the whole paint.

## Team sessions

- When a teammate drove your car, the app took the teammate for you, so your own paints and the preview were handled as theirs. You stay you; the car only tells the team.
- The team-driver preload counted a superspeedway (`_ss`) paint as ready at any track, and when Trading Paints spelled the car folder differently from iRacing, the preloaded paints were never found.
- A team driver swap seen together with a car joining or leaving now downloads the team's paints again straight away, and **Refresh now** at that moment still refreshes every driver instead of only the new ones.

## Live sessions with a replay pack

A live race was taken for a saved replay as soon as its replay data moved, which it does during any live session. With a replay pack captured for that session, new drivers then got no paints. The app now asks iRacing directly whether a saved replay is open.

## In-app updates

- The update could not start for anyone whose Windows user folder has an accented letter, such as `José`: the update script could not be written. Paths with `&`, `%`, or parentheses work too.
- When the installer failed, the app was not reopened, so a console-only service stayed off. The app now always reopens, and a version whose installer just failed is not installed automatically again for a day, so a failing installer cannot loop. **Check updates** still installs it on request.
- A pre-release such as this one can never look newer than the final release with the same number.
- An update check during an install announced the same update again, quitting during an update download still started the installer, and a failed automatic download was never offered again for that version.

## The window

- **Reset app settings** and changing the iRacing Documents folder closed the app and never reopened it, because the default install folder (`Programs\Nishizumi Paints`) has a space in it.
- Closing the window froze it for 5 seconds every time.
- A window that lost its console-only service started its own service with the settings it had when it opened, ignoring every change made since.
- Every settings change sent an update check to GitHub, which allows only 60 checks an hour.
- **Assign current** never worked for a driver's own Trading Paints paint.
- Opening the app again could stop bringing the running window forward after an unrelated program connected to its local port.
- The Session tab jumped back to the selected driver while you scrolled the list, and stuttered while paints arrived because it read the overrides and the whole paint history on every refresh. **Assign current** and **Forget** now work in the background, and the buttons of drivers being worked on stay disabled until they are done.
- **Rebuild from current files**, **Clone** and **Randomize** on the AI roster no longer freeze the window while they copy files.
- The tray icon comes back after Windows Explorer restarts.

## AI rosters

- A failed automatic roster sync at startup was never tried again while the app ran. It is now retried after 10 minutes and stops when the app stops.
- Every sync checked each spec map already on disk, about 800 requests with 20 rosters of 40 drivers. A spec map on disk is now checked at most once a day.
- `roster.json` and `notes.txt` are replaced in one step, and only when they changed, so iRacing never reads a half-written roster.
- The choice between a roster and its "(local)" copy flipped by itself after every sync.
- **Randomize** deleted the old "(random)" roster before the new one was ready, so a locked file left neither.

## Faster online fallback

- A favorite online paint replaced the list of showroom paints already fetched for that car, so every later driver started over and walked through up to 19 empty showroom pages.
- The showroom page walk never asks for a page after an empty one, and the end of a showroom is remembered for five minutes, so the next driver who joins does not ask for those pages again.
- A car Trading Paints does not list made the app download the full car catalog again right after the download that missed it, and again every minute; now at most every 15 minutes.
- The local fallback reads the random preferences and lists the RandomPool once per pass instead of once per driver.

## Other fixes

- Paint history (**Restore previous**) lost entries when several files were saved at once, and their backup files were never removed. A save retried because iRacing held the file open also filled the history with copies of the same file.
- A service restart that overlapped a slow stop could leave two copies of the service running on the same paint folder.
- A RandomPool trim by the car, helmet, or suit size limits alone reported nothing removed.
- Assigning a new car override never deleted the cache of the previous one, so every random roll stayed in `%APPDATA%` (4 to 15 MB per car set). Two assignments finishing together could also lose one of them.
- Turning the iRacing UI previews off during a session left their files in the paint folder after the session, and during a session the previews read their state file on every poll.
- Cancelling during a download left the partial file behind and could take about 20 seconds on a slow connection. A compressed paint can no longer expand past 256 MiB and fill the disk.
- A paint reload requested twice for the same car could skip the last reload.
- The iRacing Documents folder is looked up at most once a minute instead of on every poll.
- Showroom links to a paint for a grouped car, such as **Dirt Sprint Cars**, can now be imported into the RandomPool.
- Command line: `--temp-dir` is used now, `--paints-dir` (now also `--iracing-documents-dir`) is documented as the iRacing documents folder it always was, and `--session-yaml`, `--watch` and `--iracing-sdk`, which never did anything, are still accepted so old shortcuts keep working; the first two log that they are ignored.

## Validation

- 128 new tests. Every test for a fixed bug was also run against the code before its fix and fails there. They cover the host order and Team scans, the online fallback lanes and the once-per-session favorite, the mouse wheel guard, the update script with an accented and `&` path run through `cmd.exe`, the reopen-after-failure rule, the files kept with sync off and after leaving a session, the AI roster sync and file writes, the crash-proof copies and pool writes, the random fallback picks, the shared SF23 showroom list, the team preload, replay detection, the restart command, the showroom page walk, the catalog refresh, paint history under parallel saves, and the service stop and start.
- The shared showroom list, the list names and the SF23 picks were also checked against the live Trading Paints catalog.
- Full suite: 317 tests, all passing, and none of them writes to the real `%APPDATA%`.
